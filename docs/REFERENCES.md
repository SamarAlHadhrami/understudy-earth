# REFERENCES
## NASA data (core)
- LOLA LDEM/LDSM_80S_80MPP_ADJ, NASA/GSFC PGDA, pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/90.
- MGS MOLA DEM 463 m global mosaic, NASA/USGS Astropedia.
- NASA POWER (Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources), monthly and annual climatology API v2.10.1, power.larc.nasa.gov, community RE, endpoint /api/temporal/climatology/point, parameters T2M, T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN, WS2M, RH2M, PRECTOTCORR, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, 25-year climatology Jan 2001 to Dec 2025, accessed 2026-11-14. Temperature, wind and humidity (MERRA-2 based) on about 0.5 x 0.625 degree cells (about 50 km); solar (CERES SYN1deg based) on about 1 degree cells (about 110 km), per power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources/ (seen in a search excerpt; one POWER FAQ gives half-degree for some solar products). Values are regional and approximate.

Processed from ESA Mars Express HRSC stereo images (H1938_0000, H1927_0000, H1916_0000); the README states part of the work was carried out at NASA JPL under NASA contract. Downloaded directly (data/, git-ignored, 312 MB), read in geo-referenced windows (never the whole file); only derived per-window terrain numbers and small shaded-relief pictures are published (app/data/gale.json, app/data/relief/GAL*.png). Native resolution is already 30 m, so unlike Jezero there is no finer sub-check. Six 10 km windows within 90 km of the crater centre (-5.4, 137.8), chosen by lowest missing-data fraction, non-overlapping, same method as the main pool.
## Rover slope reference points (observed values, not limits)
- Curiosity maximum tilt 25.34 degrees (sol 2477) and maximum pitch 25.22 degrees (sol 2476) through sol 2488: Rankin, Maimone, Biesiadecki, Patel, Levine, Toupet, "Driving Curiosity: Mars Rover Mobility Trends During the First Seven Years", JPL, 2019 (robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/media/documents/2020-mobility-trending.pdf), Table 1. Observed peaks, not limits.
- Opportunity tilt reached 32 degrees on 2016-03-10, the steepest slope driven by any Mars rover: NASA JPL news, "Rover Takes on Steepest Slope Ever Tried on Mars", 2016-03-31, jpl.nasa.gov.
- Perseverance climbed a slope of about 20 degrees on slippery sand and dust, covering only about 50% (once 20%) of the planned distance: NASA JPL news, "NASA's Perseverance Rover Looks Back While Climbing Slippery Slope", 2024-10-28, jpl.nasa.gov.
- No official safe-driving slope limit was found or used. The slope limit in the app is user-set. An earlier set of unverified limits was removed.

## Prompt 2B sources
- Click-on-Mars grid: derived from the same NASA/USGS MGS MOLA 463 m global DEM already cited; only derived terrain numbers (200,068 windows) and a slope preview picture are published. Access: local file, no login. NASA data, derived values may be shown with credit.
- Click-on-Moon polar grid: derived from the same LOLA LDEM 80 m south-pole product already cited (NASA/GSFC PGDA); 705 windows within 300 km of the pole.
- World set: Copernicus DEM GLO-90 tiles (bucket copernicus-dem-90m, anonymous HTTPS, no login) for 45 chosen reference points, 15 km radius; licence wording not reopened: UNVERIFIED; only derived terrain numbers are published. Reference coordinates are approximate and chosen by us; no place is claimed to be an analog.
- Day-night air temperature swing: NASA POWER T2M_MAX minus T2M_MIN (already cited); context row only, not scored, not comparable with Mars or Moon surface values.
- NOT built after checking: rock-abundance / thermal-inertia proxy. Candidate products named in docs/EXTRA_FACTORS_RESEARCH.md: Mars THEMIS thermal inertia (USGS Astropedia, 100 m, within 60 S to 60 N), Moon LRO Diviner rock abundance (PDS, within 70 S to 70 N, so it does not cover the 80 S to 90 S pole data we hold), Earth MODIS day-minus-night land surface temperature (needs an Earthdata login). The three are different physical quantities on different grids, the Moon product does not cover our polar targets, and the Earth product needs a login we do not have; scoring them as one measure would be dishonest, so none was built.

**High-level summary:**
Understudy Earth is a where-and-when planner for rehearsing Moon and Mars jobs on Earth. The user picks a Moon or Mars target and describes the test (temperature, wind, dryness, sunlight needs). The app scores 10 km squares of Oman, plus two outside check sites, on terrain similarity to the target using real elevation data at one shared 500 m scale, and shows which months of the year meet the test's needs. It also says plainly where no square we searched qualifies ("no qualifying candidate in the searched area"), whether a second site closes the gap, and what Earth cannot copy. A name-free sanity check, a baseline check against random squares, a stability check and an independent second calculation test the method (none is independent validation). It helps mission planners choose practice sites and seasons with numbers, starting with Oman, which hosted the AMADEE-18 Mars analog mission in Dhofar in February 2018.

**Detailed description:**
Where: Copernicus GLO-30, LOLA south-pole and MOLA global elevation are averaged to 500 m cells and cut into 10 km squares. Each square gets slope, steepness, roughness (two scales) and relief numbers; a distance to the target is the mean of six scaled differences. Squares are ranked per target (top 5% good, 5-20% partial). A target has no qualifying candidate in the searched area when the closest Earth square is farther than the closest 20% of Earth-square pairs are from each other. Results (627 Earth squares, 17 targets): Oman alone gives 4 MATCH, 4 PARTIAL, 9 no qualifying candidate; with Meteor Crater and Atacama tiles 7 / 5 / 5. Steep Moon targets (Shackleton rim/wall, Malapert) and the Gale mound flank stay without a qualifying candidate. When: NASA POWER monthly climatology (2001-2025) gives day highs, night lows, wind, humidity and sunlight per month; each month is scored by its weakest factor against the user's needs, with a 12-month strip and best months. Where+when colouring combines both. Test needs numbers are the user's or our assumptions, not mission requirements; Earth temperatures are not Mars or Moon temperatures. Extras: Fit score (user-weighted soft score), twin shaded-relief pictures, rehearsal calendar, ESA WorldCover tree/building flag, Test Site Brief (print/PDF), AMADEE-18 replay (historical illustration with assumed needs: the planner does not rank February as best month under our assumed needs; January and December fit better, February is still a good month), typed sentence box with a rule-based matcher (no AI at runtime), compare mode, share link, confidence badge. Checks: name-free sanity check (AMADEE-18 area top 11% on average over Mars targets, 24% on best single target; Meteor Crater 4.5% / 22%; Atacama tile 51% / 25%), independent slope check agreed on 17 of 17 windows, leave-one-term-out sensitivity, automated tests. Tools: Python (numpy, scipy, rasterio, pyproj), JavaScript, Leaflet, GitHub Pages.
Baseline check: the AMADEE-18 and Meteor Crater areas rank about 20% on Mars targets versus about 50% for random squares; the Atacama tile does worse than random for Mars. Stability: 13 of 17 target tiers change under other window, cell or threshold settings. Limits: 500 m cells hide rocks and steps; rover slope limit is a rover-scale benchmark; Earth data is a surface model; Moon data covers 80S-90S only; Oman coverage is only the tiles we hold; climate is a typical-year average on coarse (about 50 km) cells; terrain match is not a geology match; no match for gravity, air or radiation.

**NASA data used:** LRO LOLA south-pole elevation and slope products; Mars Global Surveyor MOLA 463 m global DEM; NASA POWER monthly climatology.

**Space Agency partner data and references:** Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (ESA); ESA WorldCover 2021 (CC BY 4.0). Libraries: numpy, scipy, rasterio, pyproj, Playwright (testing), Leaflet. Basemap (c) OpenStreetMap contributors. AMADEE-18: Austrian Space Forum (OeWF). Rover slope reference points (observed, not limits) and all other sources: docs/REFERENCES.md.

How it works

  1. Same scale. Moon, Mars and Earth terrain models are averaged to 500 m cells and cut into 10 km squares.
  2. Terrain match. Slope, steepness, roughness at two scales and relief are compared. Each square gets a rank for the chosen target (top 5% = good, 5–20% = partial). A target has no qualifying candidate in the searched area when no square is as close as the closest 5% of Earth-square pairs are to each other (it says nothing about all of Earth).
  3. Test needs. You enter the temperature window, wind, dryness and sunlight your test needs. Each month is scored by its weakest factor.
  4. Where and when. The map combines both: a square is only GOOD if the terrain matches and a month fits.
  5. Name-free check. The scorer only sees terrain numbers, never site names. It is a sanity check, not validation.

What Earth cannot copy

  • Gravity (Mars 0.38 g, Moon 0.16 g)
  • Thin air and Martian dust, lunar vacuum
  • Radiation and real Moon/Mars temperatures
  • Geology and chemistry: a terrain match is not a rock match

What our data cannot see

  • Rocks, steps and slopes smaller than 500 m
  • Earth heights are a surface model (trees, buildings); Moon and Mars are bare ground
  • Climate is a typical-year average on a coarse grid (NASA POWER temperature, wind and humidity cells about 50 km, solar about 110 km), not local weather
  • Moon: south pole only. Oman: only the tiles we hold.

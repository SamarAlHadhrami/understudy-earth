How it works

  1. Same scale. Every terrain model (Earth 30 m, Moon 80 m, Mars 463 m) is averaged to 500 m cells, then cut into 10 km windows.
  2. Measure. For each window: slope and roughness distributions.
  3. Task. The rover task has one sourced limit: maximum slope. The share of ground under it is the “drivable share”.
  4. Score 0–100. 100 × (1 − D). D = 50% drivable-share difference + 25% slope-distribution distance + 25% roughness-distribution distance.
  5. Gap. No Earth window reaches 70 → GAP. Adding another site can lift it.
  6. Blind. The scorer only ever sees numbers, never site names.

What Earth cannot copy

  • Gravity (Mars 0.38 g, Moon 0.16 g)
  • Thin air and dust (Mars), vacuum (Moon)
  • Radiation and temperature swings
  • Geology and chemistry – a terrain match is not a rock match

What our data cannot see

  • Rocks, steps and slopes smaller than 500 m
  • Earth data is a surface model (trees, buildings); Moon and Mars are bare ground
  • Moon: south pole only (80°S–90°S). Oman: only the tiles we hold.
